Clear boundaries, honest expectations.

Trust begins with clarity. Project Suna is being designed with strict boundaries around what non-clinical support can and cannot do.

What Suna is

  • ✓
    An early research concept: An ongoing academic and design inquiry into how adults share everyday mental weight.
  • ✓
    Pseudonymous conversation exploration: Exploring ways people can converse using pseudonyms to reduce stigma, without false promises of complete anonymity.
  • ✓
    Non-clinical peer reflection: A space to organize thoughts, articulate feelings, and breathe when everything feels slightly too loud.
  • ✓
    Designed for adults 18+: Intended strictly for independent adults capable of informed participation.
  • ✓
    Zero data tracking: No tracking cookies, no accounts, and no data profiling.

What Suna is not

  • ✗
    NOT a live service: No conversational features are operational today. We do not present unbuilt tools as functional.
  • ✗
    NOT therapy or medical treatment: Suna does not diagnose, prescribe, or provide licensed mental health counseling.
  • ✗
    NOT emergency or crisis help: If you are experiencing acute distress, thoughts of self-harm, or danger, please contact emergency services or verified crisis helplines immediately.
  • ✗
    NOT "anonymous": We never use the misleading term "anonymous" and never promise absolute confidentiality, recognizing real technical and safety limits.
  • ✗
    NOT gamified: No streaks, no points, no push notifications, and no algorithmic engagement traps.
Interactive Exercise

The Suna Pause

A quiet 60-to-90 second reflection. Take a moment to step back from the rush. This exercise stores nothing, tracks nothing, and ends with an invitation to connect with someone real.

Step 1 of 4 Zero data stored

1. Arrive and soften your shoulders

Take a slow breath in, and let your jaw unclench as you exhale. You don’t have to prove anything right now. Give yourself the next 60 seconds just to sit quietly.

"Notice where your feet meet the floor. Let whatever happened before this moment rest for just a minute."

2. Acknowledge what is heavy

You don’t have to write it down, explain it, or justify it. Simply silently name the thought or feeling that has been taking up space in your chest today.

"It is completely okay if what you are feeling is messy, confusing, or contradictory."

3. You don’t have to solve everything today

When we are overwhelmed, our minds try to solve every problem at once. Give yourself permission to pause the problem-solving machine.

"You are allowed to feel exhausted without immediately having a plan to fix it."

4. Reaching out to a real person

Project Suna is an early research concept — not a person, not therapy, and not a substitute for human connection. When you feel ready, the most healing step is often reaching out to someone real.

Consider taking one small step:

Send a short, honest text to a trusted friend, family member, or care provider. (You can borrow one of our sample phrases below if finding the words feels difficult).

If you are in distress or need crisis care, please visit our Crisis Resources Directory immediately.

Research Input

Help shape Project Suna

Because Suna is in early research, we are inviting adults 18+ to share their perspective on what non-clinical conversation support should look like.

Participate in our short survey

We host our research questionnaire on an external, privacy-conscious form. We respect your emotional energy: the questionnaire takes less than 2 minutes to complete.

Fields included in this survey:

  • ● Optional contact: Email or pseudonym if you are open to a follow-up conversation.
  • ● Age verification (Required): Checkbox confirming: "I am 18 years of age or older."
  • ● Multiple-choice inquiry: One question: "Which kind of non-clinical listening space feels most missing today?" (e.g., Unstructured quiet presence, untangling a dilemma, debriefing an overwhelming day).
  • ● Interview willingness: A simple Yes / No choice regarding participation in an informal research interview.
  • ● Informed consent: Checkbox agreeing to research terms and pseudonymous data review.
Why there is NO open free-text field:

We deliberately do not provide open text boxes. This prevents the accidental submission of sensitive medical details, traumatic disclosures, or unmonitored crisis pleas that we cannot safely respond to.
